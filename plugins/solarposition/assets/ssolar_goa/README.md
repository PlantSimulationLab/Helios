# Extraterrestrial solar spectrum

`wehrli85.txt` is the extraterrestrial solar spectrum read by SolarPosition's spectral solar irradiance model (`calculateGlobalSolarSpectrum()`, `calculateDirectSolarSpectrum()`, `calculateDiffuseSolarSpectrum()`) and by `calculateSensorAtmosphereSpectra()`. It is an unmodified copy of the 1985 Wehrli standard extraterrestrial solar irradiance spectrum (Wehrli, C., 1985, Extraterrestrial Solar Spectrum, Publication no. 615, Physikalisch-Meteorologisches Observatorium and World Radiation Center, Davos), as distributed by the National Laboratory of the Rockies (formerly the National Renewable Energy Laboratory) at https://www.nlr.gov/grid/solar-resource/spectra-wehrli. It is distributed subject to the laboratory's data notice, reproduced in `NLR_DATA_NOTICE.txt`, which asks that the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE)/NLR/ALLIANCE be credited in any publication that results from the use of the data.

After a header, each line of the file gives a wavelength (nm), the spectral irradiance at the mean Earth-Sun distance (W m-2 nm-1) and the cumulative irradiance (W m-2). The wavelengths are not evenly spaced: 1 nm apart up to about 630 nm (0.4 nm apart from 310 to 330.4 nm), 2 nm apart up to about 1000 nm, 5 nm apart up to about 2000 nm, 10-25 nm apart up to about 2640 nm (25 nm near 2600 nm), and wider beyond, up to 2290 nm at the 10075 nm end. When the file is loaded (`SolarPosition::SpectralData::loadFromFile()`), each value is taken to apply halfway to the neighbouring wavelengths, and the resulting piecewise-constant spectrum is averaged over 1 nm bins centered on 300, 301, ..., 2600 nm. `plugins/solarposition/utilities/wehrli_spectrum.py` applies the same averaging for the validation utilities.

Gas absorption for the spectral model comes from the gas transmittance tables of `../atmosphere_lut/atmosphere_lut.bin` (see its README).
